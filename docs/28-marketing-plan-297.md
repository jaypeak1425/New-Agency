## 1. Positioning

**Category:** Not "another BGA." Not "another SaaS." The category we create:
**a producer-development firm with a brain you can subscribe to.**

**Positioning statement:** For insurance agents already producing $100K+ who want to become
million-dollar producers, PeakBritt is the advanced-planning agency where you're mentored
one-on-one by producers actually writing at that level — and Case Atlas is their case-design
brain in software, so you can *think like us* on every case. Unlike BGAs that hand you another
contract, a seminar, or a lead program, this is real mentorship, not marketing hype.

**The three sentences every asset orbits:**
1. "You don't need more leads. You need bigger cases."
2. "Watch the software think." (the demo IS the ad)
3. "Contract with PeakBritt and the software comes at no cost — plus a million-dollar producer in your
   corner, one-on-one."

**Price architecture (anchoring):** $297/mo is real and defensible on its own ROI story (one
found case pays for years of it). Its second job is to make "no cost with contract" land as a
$3,564/year gift that signals how much PeakBritt invests in its producers — the reciprocity
trigger that opens the contracting conversation.

## 2. ICP — the one avatar for Stage 1

**"The Plateaued Producer."**
- Licensed life/annuity agent or advisor, 3–15 years in, **earning $100K–$250K/yr** personal
  commission. Independent or loosely captive; owns their book.
- Writes mostly term, IUL, final expense, or simple annuities on activity volume. Sees
  business owners and affluent clients in their book but **doesn't have the case-design skill
  to open the big conversation** — and knows it.
- Has bought courses, joined a "producer group," run Facebook-lead programs. Burned by all
  three. Skeptical of anything that smells like recruiting.
- Emotional state (verified against the failure research in §3): **capable, stuck, and
  alone.** Proud of surviving an industry most people wash out of; quietly aware the next
  level requires something nobody around them has.
- Watering holes: insurance-agent Facebook groups & YouTube, LinkedIn, industry podcasts,
  carrier conferences, study groups.

**Explicit non-ICP for Stage 1:** brand-new/unlicensed agents (no book, wrong economics),
sub-$100K producers (activity problem, not case-size problem), and IMO principals (Phase-5
lane). Ads must repel these as clearly as they attract the ICP — exclusivity starts in the
targeting.

## 3. Why agents fail — research the whole plan is built on

The industry's open secrets, and what each means for our message:

| Finding | The reality | Message implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Catastrophic early washout** | Industry retention studies consistently put new-agent failure at roughly 30%+ in year one and ~90% within the first 3–5 years. The model recruits mass and develops few. | Our ICP are *survivors*. Compliment the survival ("you made it further than 9 in 10"), then name the ceiling. |
| **Commission-only pressure → product-pushing** | Agents learn to sell what closes fast, not what the client's situation actually calls for. Skill ossifies at the simple-product level. | "You were taught to sell products. Nobody taught you to design cases." |
| **The $100K plateau is structural** | $100K ≈ personal-activity maximum. Million-dollar producers don't work 10x harder; they write 10–20x cases via advanced planning (buy-sell, §162, ILIT, qualified-money design). That knowledge lives in a tiny circle and is not taught. | The gap is knowable and closable — "think like us." Software makes the claim demonstrable in 60 seconds. |
| **Isolation is the emotional core** | No producing mentor. Nobody to call with "I've got a guy…". Study groups share war stories, not case designs. Loneliness compounds into stagnation and exit — agents describe feeling like "an island." | Lead ads with the moment: *you, alone at your desk at 9pm with a case you don't know how to design.* Then answer it: a desk that answers, a mentor who produces. |
| **"Support" is a commodity lie** | Every BGA promises support; delivery is a contract, a product sheet, a quarterly seminar, maybe leads. The agent's actual cases still get designed alone. | Never say "support." Show the artifact: the case design, the handoff email, the deck — generated in front of them. |
| **Recruiting fatigue** | The ICP gets recruiting DMs weekly. Pattern-matched and auto-ignored. | Sell the software first (a purchase, not a pledge). Contracting is *applied for*, capped, and refusable — the opposite shape of a recruiting pitch. |

**Why our offer changes it (the paragraph every asset compresses):** Every other path asks the
stuck agent to fix themselves with more of what created the plateau — more leads, more calls,
more seminars. This offer changes the two variables that actually bind: **knowledge** (Case
Atlas puts million-dollar case design in their hands tonight) and **isolation** (a producing
mentor and a named case desk in their corner). And it proves both *before* asking for
commitment: the ad demos the software, the trial delivers it, and only then does the
contract conversation start.

## 4. The psychology & NLP framework

How the pitch is engineered. Every technique below maps to specific copy in docs/29 — and one
rule governs all of it: **every claim must be literally true and deliverable.** Persuasion
architecture, never fabrication; that's what "exclusive and real" means.

**Influence principles (Cialdini):**
- **Reciprocity** — no-cost case-design webinars; the $3,564/yr software gift inside the contract.
- **Commitment/consistency** — micro-commitments: watch demo → trial → run one live case →
  application. Each step self-identifies them as "a producer who invests in craft."
- **Social proof** — anonymized case wins ("a producer in Ohio found a $40K case in a book
  he'd owned for six years"), cohort membership, waitlist.
- **Authority** — mentors are *producing* million-dollar writers; the software encodes their
  actual thinking; tax-law citations in agent-facing views.
- **Liking** — founder-voice copy ("Real talk"), naming their private experience precisely.
- **Scarcity (real)** — 25 Founding Producer seats, application + interview + production
  floor. We publicly decline applicants. Never fake counters.
- **Loss aversion** — "the case you missed last month was worth more than this software costs
  in a decade" / "every month at the plateau costs you the delta."
- **Anchoring** — $297 anchors no-cost-with-contract; $10K average case anchors $297 as trivial.
- **Identity (the deepest lever)** — sell the transition from "agent who sells policies" to
  "advisor who designs cases." People act to become who they believe they are.

**NLP techniques (ethically applied):**
- **Presuppositions** — "When you run your first case tonight…" (assumes signup); "Which of
  your clients comes to mind?" (assumes the case exists — it always does).
- **Future pacing** — walk them into the next meeting: "You walk in with the deck, the design,
  the order of operations. The meeting isn't a discovery call. It's a presentation."
- **Embedded commands** — imperative phrases inside sentences: "producers who *run the system*
  break through"; "you can *think like us* on every case."
- **Pattern interrupt** — ads open against genre: "Stop buying leads." / "This is not a
  recruiting pitch." Interrupts the auto-ignore reflex the ICP has built.
- **Pacing and leading** — state three things they already know are true (verifiable pain from
  §3), *then* introduce the new idea. Truth earns the lead.
- **Reframing** — the plateau reframed from personal failure to structural: "You didn't stall.
  Your toolset did."
- **Sensory-specific language** — "9pm, kitchen table, a legal pad with a case you don't know
  how to design" — the movie of their real life, not abstractions.
- **Anaphora / rhythmic repetition** — "Real mentorship. Real cases. Real overrides. Not
  marketing hype." (also the brand's central contrast frame).

**The master frame:** *Belonging + Becoming.* The subtext of every asset: "You've been doing
this alone. You don't have to. And the person you'd be mentored by actually does what you want
to do." Software = proof; mentorship = relationship; contract = belonging.

## 5. SWOT

**Strengths**
- Working, demonstrable product — the demo closes; nobody else's pitch can be *watched*.
- Founders/mentors genuinely produce at the level being sold (authority is real).
- Two-lane model: SaaS cash flow + override upside; no-cost-with-contract is a moat competitors
  with pure-SaaS or pure-BGA economics can't cheaply copy.
- Compliance engineered in (3-layer guardrail) — a real E&O talking point for recruits.
- Tiny and fast: offer, price, and curriculum can change in a week.

**Weaknesses**
- No public brand or track record; zero testimonials at launch.
- Founder-calendar dependency in mentorship delivery (mitigation: Stage-2 productization).
- Single-digit team; Tracy not yet licensed at Stage-1 start.
- $10K-average-case and 20%-override assumptions unvalidated at scale.
- Software depends on continued carrier/product breadth PeakBritt can actually wholesale.

**Opportunities**
- The mid-career plateaued producer is *underserved*: incumbents chase either new-agent mass
  recruiting or elite established teams — the middle is nobody's ICP.
- AI-assisted case design is new enough that first-mover credibility is available in the
  agent-facing niche.
- OBBBA-era tax complexity (AMT trap, permanence of exemptions) creates demand for
  advanced-planning literacy the average agent lacks.
- Massive experienced-agent dissatisfaction with lead-program churn = receptive audience.
- Later: annual plans, IMO white-label (Phase 5), courses/community as mid-funnel products.

**Threats**
- Big BGAs/IMOs (see §7) could bolt AI tooling onto vast recruiting machines and budgets.
- Carrier/AI-platform policy shifts affecting product economics or the engine.
- Copycat "AI case design" claims cheapening the category before we own it.
- Regulatory attention to AI-generated insurance recommendations (mitigated by Brain-lock +
  compliance filter, but the narrative risk exists).
- A defection or public dispute with an early contracted producer (small-roster risk).

**SWOT→action:** lead every asset with the demo (S1) into the underserved middle (O1); cap and
gate contracting so weaknesses (W2, W3) become the exclusivity story; instrument case economics
from day one (W4); build testimonial capture into the 90-day sprint so W1 dies by month 6.

## 6. Funnel & channels (Stage 1)

**Funnel:** Ad (software demo) → landing page (watch Atlas think → trial CTA) → $297 trial
(30-day money-back) → in-app "aha" (first case design; a case-desk touch from Luke or Jay) → invite
webinar / 1-on-1 case review → **Founding Producer application** → interview → contract →
no-cost software + 90-day mentorship sprint → placed cases → referrals + testimonials → loop.

**Working targets (measure and re-set monthly):** landing→trial 5–8% · trial→retained sub
60%+ · sub→application 15% over 90 days · application→contract ~50% (we decline half —
enforced selectivity) · contracted→producing ≥80% by day 90.

**Channels, in priority order:**
1. **Meta + YouTube paid** (the docs/29 demo commercials, delivered by the front man —
   docs/27 §8) — interest/lookalike targeting on agent signals; YouTube pre-roll on
   insurance-sales content. Budget $3–6K/mo Stage 1.
2. **Front-man organic** — LinkedIn + short-form: one real case design/week ("I've got a
   guy" → screen recording → design), fronted by the agency's voice with Luke/Jay cameos as
   the producing mentors. The content IS the product demo.
3. **Webinars (2/mo)** — "Design a million-dollar producer's case live." Front man hosts;
   Luke or Jay designs. Reciprocity engine + application harvest.
4. **CPE seminars → CPA/COI network (docs/27 §7)** — Luke and Jay teach CPAs advanced-planning
   topics for CE credit; the room becomes the referral network that feeds Channel-3 personal
   production ($50K avg cases). The same seminar system is packaged as a mentorship module so
   contracted producers build their own COI referral engines (docs/29 §10).
5. **Direct outreach** — 20 hand-picked plateaued producers/mo (docs/29 DM/email scripts).
   High-touch lane that also pressure-tests the pitch.
6. **Email** — the existing 7-part sequence (docs/15, now at $297) for non-buyers; a parallel
   5-part contracting sequence for subscribers (docs/29 §6).
7. **Referrals (from month 4)** — "who's the best producer you know who's stuck?" wired into
   the mentorship sprint's day-60 session.

**Measurement:** CAC per channel, sub→contract rate, cost per contracted producer, placed
cases/mo, average commission/case — reviewed monthly against docs/27 §4 math; kill or scale
channels on 90-day CAC payback.

| **Ash Brokerage** | Large independent BGA, respected underwriting desk | Wholesaling + case consultation | Good case help *when you ask right* | Their help starts at the illustration; ours starts at "I've got a guy." |
| **DBS (Diversified Brokerage Services)** | National life BGA | Traditional wholesaling | Contract + case desk | Same pattern: reactive desk vs. proactive brain + mentor. |
| **M Financial Group** | Elite producer-owned consortium; proprietary products | Member-firm model for the top 0.5% | The best economics in the industry — if you're invited | Aspirational proof our category exists at the top; we're the on-ramp for the not-yet-invited. |
| **LIBRA Insurance Partners / AIMCOR Group** | Large BGA networks/consortiums | Aggregated scale for member BGAs | B2B infrastructure | Invisible to the individual agent; not competing for their heart. |
| **TruChoice / Gradient / Annexus-style FMOs** | Annuity-centric FMOs | Leads/seminar programs + contracts | The seminar-and-leads loop | This is exactly the "latest seminar or Facebook ads" the brand defines itself against. |

**Pattern:** every incumbent sells *distribution* (contracts, products, marketing programs).
None sells *transformation* (skill + mentorship + a thinking tool), and none can demo their
value in a 60-second screen recording. That's the open lane, and the moat is that our
economics (override on production we help create) align us with the agent's growth, not just
their paper.

## 8. Brand voice

- **Founder-direct.** First person, short sentences, "Real talk." No corporate we-speak.
- **Respectful of the ICP's competence.** They're survivors, not marks. Never condescend.
- **Show, don't claim.** Every asset contains or points to an artifact: a case design, a deck,
  a handoff email.
- **Anti-hype as an aesthetic.** The category screams; we demonstrate. "Real mentorship, not
  marketing hype" is a voice instruction, not just a tagline.
- **Named humans.** The front man carries the public voice (docs/27 §8); Luke and Jay appear
  as the producing mentors; Tracy holds the contracts behind the scenes. Institutions are the
  competition's voice.
