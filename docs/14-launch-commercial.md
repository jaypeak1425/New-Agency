## The 60-Second Version

### Scene 1: The Setup (0:00-0:08)
**Visual:** A producer in their car, parked outside a client's office. The engine is in the ignition. They're on their phone. Premium cinematography — not stock-feeling. Warm light. Quiet confidence.

**Voiceover (Jay, calm Adventurer voice):**
"So you've just left an appointment. You're in your car. And you have a thought."

**On-screen text:** "I've got a guy."

### Scene 2: The Demo (0:08-0:25)
**Visual:** Screen recording of the agent opening Case Atlas on their phone. They tap the new scenario button. They type (or speak):

*"Two owners 50 and 49, C-Corp, two key employees, average to good health. Want to set up a buy-sell and put money aside in a company reserve."*

Atlas asks 10 questions, conversational, one at a time. The agent answers. The screen shows the questions and the answers flowing.

**Voiceover:**
"You open Case Atlas. You type 'I've got a guy.' Atlas asks you the right questions."

**On-screen text:** Atlas is asking. The agent is answering.

### Scene 3: The Output (0:25-0:45)
**Visual:** Atlas returns the case design. The strategy stack appears:
- Buy-sell (with the order of operations)
- COLI (with the §101(j) notice and consent flagged)
- §162 Executive Bonus / REBA (with the vesting schedule)
- Phantom stock (with the equity-aligned retention)
- Estate Funding (future conversation flag)

The pitch deck appears. The wholesaler handoff email appears. The COI action appears. The estimated commission value appears: **$42,900 in Y1 commission.**

**Voiceover:**
"In 60 seconds, you get the full case design. The strategies. The pitch order. The pitch deck. The wholesaler handoff. The COI actions. The estimated commission value."

**On-screen text:** "$42,900 in Y1 commission on this scenario."

### Scene 4: The Walk-In (0:45-0:55)
**Visual:** The producer walks into the next meeting. They open their tablet. The pitch deck is on the screen. The two business owners across the table look engaged. The producer is calm, prepared, leading the meeting.

**Voiceover:**
"You walk into the next meeting with everything ready. The next meeting isn't a discovery call. It's a presentation."

**On-screen text:** "The next meeting isn't a discovery call. It's a presentation."

### Scene 5: The Close (0:55-1:00)
**Visual:** Premium black background. Gold accent line. The brand mark appears.

**Voiceover:**
"The strategy engine for the producer who's done guessing."

**On-screen text:**
"Case Atlas"
"$297/month. 30-day money-back."

## Music Direction

**Style:** Premium, calm, slightly cinematic. Not upbeat, not hype. Think: Apple keynote music, Goldman Sachs brand video, a quiet confidence.

**Tempo:** Slow to medium (60-80 BPM)

**Instrumentation:** Soft piano or strings. Minimal. No drums. No vocals.

**Reference tracks:**
- Apple "Product Red" launch music
- Goldman Sachs "Progress is everyone's business" brand video music
- A premium SaaS product launch (Linear, Vercel, Notion)

**Volume:** Low under voiceover. Rises slightly during the output reveal (Scene 3) to add emotional weight.

## Production Checklist

- [ ] Hire a premium video production company (or contractor) with experience in B2B SaaS brand videos
- [ ] Cast a real producer for the actor (not a model) — or use a real producer if comfortable on camera
- [ ] Shoot the in-car scene in a real car, real location, real lighting (not studio)
- [ ] Shoot the meeting scene in a real office, real people, real setup (not a green screen)
- [ ] Record screen captures of the actual engine output (or a high-fidelity mockup if the engine isn't built yet)
- [ ] Record Jay's voiceover in a professional studio (or high-quality home setup)
- [ ] Edit for premium feel — slow cuts, generous pauses, no jumpy transitions
- [ ] Color grade for premium palette (deep navy, warm gold, soft cream per Brand Package)
- [ ] Sound design — clean audio, subtle ambient sounds in the car and meeting scenes
- [ ] Music licensing — original composition or licensed track with full usage rights
- [ ] Final review for compliance — every frame, every voiceover line, every on-screen word passes the brand rules check
- [ ] Export in 1080p (for digital) and 4K (for events / press)
- [ ] Cut-downs for paid ads (15s, 30s) using the same footage

## Compliance Check (Frame by Frame)

- [x] No guarantee language
- [x] No specific income claims (the $42,900 is the scenario value, not the agent's actual income)
- [x] No "tax-free" (use "non-taxable" if any client copy appears)
- [x] No "Firm Advantage" or "CPA on staff" implication
- [x] No IRS form numbers in client-facing copy
- [x] The promise is based on the agent's own book
- [x] The voice is honest, plain-spoken, calm
- [x] No fake urgency ("limited time," "act now," etc.)
- [x] No guru language
- [x] The price is transparent and the guarantee is structured as a money-back, not a result guarantee
- [x] The demo shows a realistic scenario, not an exaggerated one
