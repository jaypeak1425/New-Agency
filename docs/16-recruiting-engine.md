# Recruiting Engine Spec

## Purpose
The Recruiting Engine is the marketing system that brings agents and IMOs in. It's not the product itself — it's the assets and funnels that market the product. The system has two halves: the individual agent marketing (drives sign-ups to the $97/mo direct offer) and the IMO marketing (drives white-label contracts at $75/seat).

**This doc is what the marketing team and the developer build against.** It includes the per-avatar offer stack, the per-avatar channel mix, the asset list per segment, the launch playbooks, the KPIs, and the integration with the master dashboard.

---

## 1. The 4 Marketing Principles (Bake Into Every Asset)

Every asset in the Recruiting Engine follows these 4 principles:

### Principle 1: The iPhone Moment
The marketing demos the product, not describes it. The agent or IMO principal sees the engine work in real time before they're asked to consider buying. The "you're in your car after an appointment, you open the app, you type 'I've got a guy,' the engine returns the full case design" is the centerpiece of every demo, every sales page, every commercial.

### Principle 2: Elegant, Rich, Defensible
The brand vibe is premium. The visual is high-end. The copy is confident but not hyped. The promise is based on the prospect's actual book of business, not invented numbers. No "guaranteed $1M." No "we'll make you rich." Just "look at the opportunities in your book."

### Principle 3: One Promise, Plainly Stated
Every asset leads with one clear promise:
- For individual agents: "Find the opportunities already in your book"
- For IMOs: "Give your agents a strategy engine they can't get anywhere else"
- For the discovery call: the demo is the promise

The promise is the headline, the subject line, the first 3 words of the ad. Everything else supports it.

### Principle 4: Honest, Plain, Adventurer Voice
Jay's voice. Honest, plain-spoken, calm. No guru language. No "unlock your potential." No "transform your business." Just "here's the engine, here's what it does, here's how it works."

## 2. The Per-Avatar Offer Stack (Individual Agents)

The $97/mo direct offer is positioned differently for each avatar:

### For the Stuck Producer ($100K+ for 3-5 years)
**The promise:** "Find the opportunities in your book you've been missing."
**The hook:** "You're at $100K. The producers at $1M aren't smarter than you. They have a system. Here's the system."
**The CTA:** "Run your first 'I've got a guy' — see what the engine finds."
**The asset:** The 5-min demo video, the soap-opera email sequence, the Dream 100 of producers.

### For the RIA Who Won't Sell Insurance
**The promise:** "Add insurance revenue without adding the operational lift."
**The hook:** "You know the insurance opportunity is in your client base. You don't want to deal with the underwriting, the products, the wholesalers. The engine does the case design. You make the introduction. We do the rest."
**The CTA:** "See the engine handle a scenario in 60 seconds."
**The asset:** The RIA-specific pitch, the COI workflow, the "we handle the heavy lifting" positioning.

### For the New Producer (less than 3 years)
**The promise:** "Skip the 5-year plateau. Start with a system, not just a product catalog."
**The hook:** "You're learning the business. The producers who break through fast have a system. The producers who plateau are guessing. Here's the system, and it's $97/mo."
**The CTA:** "Try it for 30 days. If you don't find an opportunity in your first month, money back."
**The asset:** The 30-day guarantee positioning, the "first-year fast track" angle.

## 4. The IMO Marketing Stack (White-Label at $75/Seat)

The IMO marketing is fundamentally a B2B sale. The buyer is the IMO principal, not the individual agent. The IMO principal is buying a retention tool, a recruitment tool, and a production tool to offer to their downline.

### The IMO Buyer Psychology

The IMO principal's pain:
- Their agents are leaving for the next shiny thing (other IMOs, other products, other opportunities)
- Their top agents are getting poached by competitors
- Their average agent production is flat or declining
- They don't have a way to differentiate their tech stack from every other IMO
- They can't retain producers who are stuck at $100K-$200K

The IMO's gain from the engine:
- A retention tool that keeps their downline (once an agent uses the engine, they can't un-experience it)
- A recruiting tool that helps them attract new agents ("we have the strategy engine that no one else has")
- A production tool that increases the average case size of their downline

## 6. The 3 Paid Ad Templates (Cold Audiences)

**Template 1: The "iPhone moment" ad**
- 15-30 second video
- Shows the agent typing "I've got a guy" and the engine returning the case design
- Caption: "The strategy engine in your pocket. $97/mo."
- CTA: "See it work in 60 seconds."

**Template 2: The "stuck at $100K" ad**
- Static or short video
- Headline: "You're at $100K. The producers at $1M aren't smarter."
- Subhead: "They have a system. Here's the system."
- CTA: "Try it for 30 days."

**Template 3: The "I've got a guy" ad**
- Short video
- Shows the agent in their car, opening the app, typing the scenario
- "What's in your book that you haven't seen yet?"
- CTA: "Find out."

**Compliance note for all paid ads:**
- No "guaranteed income" claims
- No "we'll make you a millionaire"
- Prominent disclosure (ad is for a software product, not a financial product)
- "Past results don't guarantee future performance" or equivalent
- The platform's ad review process must approve every ad before spend

## 7. The 3 Launch Playbooks

### Playbook 1: The Soft Launch (months 4-6, ~50 paying agents)
- Email the 5-7 email sequence to the design partners
- Post 3x/week on LinkedIn and Instagram in Adventurer voice
- Run 1 paid ad (the "stuck at $100K" template) at $500/month budget
- Reach out to 20 IMO principals with the LinkedIn script (no pitch, just connect)
- Show the product at 1 industry event
- Get 3 case studies from design partners

### Playbook 2: The Public Launch (months 7-9, IMO outreach begins)
- The 60-90 sec demo video goes public on YouTube, LinkedIn, Instagram
- The IMO pitch deck is finalized
- The Dream 100 IMO outreach begins (personalized emails to 100 IMO principals)
- The launch commercial is produced and distributed
- The paid ad budget scales to $2,000-$5,000/month
- 5 industry events attended

### Playbook 3: The Scale (months 10-12, $30K MRR target)
- The IMO pipeline is active (10-15 active IMO conversations)
- The first mid-size IMO contract is signed
- The case studies are published (3-5 IMOs with results)
- The paid ad budget scales to $5,000-$10,000/month
- A sales hire is added (IMO-focused)
- 10-15 industry events attended

## 8. The 5 KPIs (How the Recruiting Engine is Measured)

| KPI | What it measures | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost per signup** | Marketing spend ÷ new signups | <$150 |
| **Activation rate** | % of signups who run first scenario in 7 days | >60% |
| **Free-to-paid conversion** | % of trial users who convert to paid | >25% |
| **IMO pipeline value** | Total annual contract value of active IMO conversations | $500K by month 9 |
| **Channel attribution** | Which channels drive signups | Multi-channel (no single channel >50%) |

## 9. The 3 Edge Cases

**Edge case 1: An agent signs up but never activates.**
The activation nudge sequence kicks in (3 emails over 14 days, then a personal outreach from the team if no activation). If the agent still doesn't activate, they're flagged for churn risk in the master dashboard.

**Edge case 2: An IMO principal signs the pilot but their agents don't adopt.**
The IMO activation sequence kicks in (the IMO principal gets a "how to drive adoption" playbook, the team supports the IMO with in-app announcements, the per-seat usage dashboard is monitored weekly). If adoption stays low after 30 days, the team works directly with the IMO principal to diagnose.

**Edge case 3: A paid ad gets rejected by the platform.**
The ad is reworked to remove the violating element (typically a guarantee claim or a financial outcome claim). The compliance officer reviews before resubmission. The marketing team keeps a log of platform rejections to learn the patterns.
