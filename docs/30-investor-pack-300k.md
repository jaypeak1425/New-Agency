## 2. The business in one paragraph

PeakBritt Financial Group is a boutique advanced-planning insurance agency whose edge is that
its principals produce at the level its recruits want to reach. Case Atlas is that edge in
software: a locked-library case-design engine any licensed agent can run. Revenue comes through
three channels: the software at **$297/month**; a **20% override** on recruited agents'
production (**~$2,000 per placed case**); and **Luke and Jay's personal production** at a
**90% commission contract** on an **average $50,000 sale — roughly $45,000 per personal case**.
The software is the hook (agents can watch it think); the contract is the upsell (software at
no cost + one-on-one mentorship from million-dollar producers); personal production funds and
de-risks the transition. Stage-1 goal: **$50K/month total revenue within 6 months** —
achievable with one personal case per month while the recurring channels ramp underneath.

## 4. The three sales channels (unit economics)

| Channel | Economics | Year-1 role |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Software** | $297/mo (annual $2,970); ~90% gross margin | Lead engine + recurring base; every subscriber is a warm contracting prospect |
| **2 — Override** | 20% × $10K avg target commission = **$2K/placed case**; ~95% gross margin | The scalable business; capped, gated, application-only (exclusivity is real) |
| **3 — Personal production** | 90% × $50K avg sale ≈ **$45K/case**, sourced via the CPA/CPE network | The bridge: one case/month ≈ the entire Stage-1 goal |

The funnel: demo-led ads → $297 subscribers → in-app "aha" → Founding Producer application →
contract (software at no cost + mentorship) → override revenue → referrals. The CPE seminar
system (docs/27 §7) builds the CPA referral network that feeds Channel 3 — and is then taught
to contracted producers, which is the retention moat.

## 5. The load-bearing assumption — named, not buried

The model's biggest number is Channel 3: **$45K of revenue per personal case, ~1/month.**
Everything else (subscriptions, overrides) compounds slowly and predictably; personal
production is what makes Year 1 cash-positive. An investor should verify it directly:
founders' prior case sizes, the 90% contract in writing, and the CPA-referral pipeline. The
downside case in §6 shows the business at HALF the personal production — still viable, slower.
The second assumption worth pressure-testing: $10K average target commission on recruited-agent
cases (drives the $2K override). Both averages are instrumented in the product's dashboard from
case #1, so the model self-corrects monthly.

## 8. Proposed terms + the investor's operating role

**Proposed structure (for negotiation):** $300,000 via SAFE, $2.75M post-money valuation cap
(≈10.9% as-converted), standard terms. Alternative: priced equity round at the same valuation.
Board observer seat; information rights (monthly KPI pack, quarterly financials).

**The front-man role (the strategic part of this deal):** During the transition —
**the investor serves as the public voice of PeakBritt**: delivering the docs/29 commercials,
hosting the twice-monthly case-design webinars, and fronting the YouTube/LinkedIn content,
while Luke and Jay stay pointed at production and mentorship (their calendars monetize at
~$45K/case, not on camera). Formalized in a **services agreement** alongside the investment:
defined deliverables (shoot days/month, webinars/month), a defined transition window
(target months 1–9, extendable by mutual agreement), and a succession plan (the role converts
to a hired voice as Stage-2 revenue supports it). An advisor option grant (0.5–1.0%, vesting
monthly over the service period) compensates the services separately from the invested capital
— clean cap table, clean expectations.

**Magellan Financial in-kind contribution:** video production and marketing resources —
studio/shoot capability, editing, creative support — are provided by Magellan Financial at no
cash cost to PeakBritt. Effect on the model: the $300K buys **distribution** (media spend)
rather than production overhead, roughly doubling effective marketing throughput per dollar.
Scope, usage rights, and brand separation (Magellan is never named in consumer/agent-facing
assets) are documented in the same services agreement.

**Why this alignment works:** the investor's stake appreciates on exactly the metrics the
front-man role drives (subscribers, applications, contracted producers), and the founders'
time is protected for the channel that pays for everything in Year 1. Everyone is paid by the
same scoreboard.

## 9. Risks — the honest list

1. **Key-person concentration (the big one):** Year-1 economics ride on Luke and Jay's
   production. Mitigations: the entire Stage 2 plan converts revenue to recurring channels;
   key-person insurance on both founders (we sell this for a living); the $80K working-capital
   buffer absorbs case lumpiness.
2. **Personal-production averages miss:** $50K avg case / 1 per month may run lighter or
   slower. Mitigation: the downside case is funded; the CPA/CPE pipeline is a volume lever
   that starts in month 1.
3. **Recruiting conversion unproven:** sub→contract at 15%/90 days is an estimate until
   measured. Mitigation: founder-led sales carry the first 10 contracts; the funnel is fully
   instrumented; the cap (25) means the target is 10 producers, not 1,000.
4. **Chargebacks/persistency:** early policy lapses claw back commissions. Mitigation:
   advanced-planning cases (trust-owned, professionally advised) persist far better than
   transactional business; reserve held in working capital.
5. **Regulatory/AI narrative risk:** AI-generated insurance recommendations draw scrutiny.
   Mitigation: the Brain is locked (no AI invention), every client-facing output passes a
   compliance filter, everything is audit-logged — this is the best-defended posture in the
   category and a diligence exhibit, not a liability.
6. **Transition risk on the front-man role itself:** if the arrangement ends early, content
   stalls. Mitigation: defined deliverables in the services agreement, a library of evergreen
   demo-led assets banked in the first 90 days, and the Stage-2 hired-voice succession plan.

$650K–$1M (**2.2–3.3×**), before any dividend/distribution policy on a cash-generative
pass-through — which this becomes in Year 1 under plan. The faster path to liquidity in this
industry is strategic acquisition: distribution roll-ups pay for producer rosters and
proprietary tech, and this asset is deliberately built with both.

and average commission per case (both channels) · personal-production pipeline (CPA referrals

cash position vs. runway plan.
