## The 5 IMO Segments (How the List Is Organized)

### Segment 1: The Tier-1 National IMOs (10-15 principals)
**Description:** The largest IMO/FMOs in the country, with 1,000+ agents in their downline. National brand recognition. Often have a marketing team, a tech stack, an internal training program.

**Examples of the type:** [Tier-1 IMO names — to be populated in Pass 2]

**Why this segment matters:** Landing one Tier-1 IMO is a 7-figure annual contract. They have the budget, the decision-making authority, and the credibility to move fast.

**The outreach angle:** *"You're already giving your agents a CRM, a lead vendor, and a marketing program. What if you could give them a strategy engine that no other IMO has? Recruiting, retention, and revenue — in one product. White-label as your own."*

### Segment 2: The Mid-Market Specialist IMOs (30-40 principals)
**Description:** IMOs with 100-500 agents, often specializing in a niche (annuities, life, senior market, business market, estate planning, etc.). Strong principal relationships, less marketing muscle than Tier-1.

**Examples of the type:** [Mid-market IMO names — to be populated in Pass 2]

**Why this segment matters:** These are the IMO principals who feel the recruitment and retention pain most acutely. They have the downline size to be a real contract, but not the bureaucracy to move slowly.

**The outreach angle:** *"You're in the [niche] market. Your agents are the [producer type]. The engine is built for them — case design that finds the opportunities in [niche] scenarios. White-label, your brand, your downline."*

### Segment 3: The Regional / Local IMOs (30-40 principals)
**Description:** IMOs with 25-100 agents, often state-focused or region-focused. Strong relationships, tight downlines, the principal often knows every agent by name.

**Examples of the type:** [Regional IMO names — to be populated in Pass 2]

**Why this segment matters:** These are the IMO principals who will adopt fast. Small enough to make a decision in a week, big enough to be a real contract, tight enough that the white-label rollout lands personally.

**The outreach angle:** *"You know your downline by name. You know who's stuck at $100K and who could break through with the right system. The engine is that system. White-label, your brand, your downline. Try 50 seats for 90 days."*

### Segment 4: The Independent Marketing Organizations (10-15 principals)
**Description:** Smaller, independent organizations that focus on producer development. Often led by senior producers who built a following and started an IMO. Strong culture, often tech-forward.

**Examples of the type:** [Independent IMO names — to be populated in Pass 2]

**Why this segment matters:** These IMOs are early adopters. They want the tech, they want the differentiation, they want the recruiting story. They're the first ones in the door.

**The outreach angle:** *"You're the IMO that's built on the producer-development story. The engine is the next chapter of that story. White-label, your brand, your downline. Be the IMO that has it first."*

### Segment 5: The BGA / GA / MGA Network (5-10 principals)
**Description:** The legacy BGAs, GAs, and MGAs that have been around for decades. Often family-run, often conservative, often slow to adopt new tech — but loyal to their downline and well-respected in the industry.

**Examples of the type:** [BGA/GA/MGA names — to be populated in Pass 2]

**Why this segment matters:** These are the IMO principals with the longest tenured downlines. If they adopt, their agents stay for decades. The recruiting story is "the BGA that's been around 30 years just adopted the strategy engine."

**The outreach angle:** *"You've been in this business for [X] years. You know the strategies. Your agents know the strategies. The engine puts all of it in their pocket, with the rules baked in, the compliance built in, and the COI workflow automated. White-label, your brand, your downline."*

## The Outreach Campaign Sequence

For each principal on the list, the outreach follows this sequence:

### Step 1: Research (1-2 hours per principal)
- Find the principal's name, title, and direct contact
- Read the IMO's website, their LinkedIn, their recent press
- Identify the IMO's niche, their downline size, their tech stack (if visible)
- Note the personalized angle (which of the 5 segments, which of the 5 outreach angles applies)

### Step 2: LinkedIn Connection Request (Day 1)
**Template:** "Hi [Name], I noticed Peakbritt Financial Group focuses on [niche]. We've built something that addresses [pain point] for IMOs in your space. Would love to connect."

**Compliance check:** No guarantee language. No specific income claims. The connection request is one sentence, no pitch.

### Step 3: Follow-Up Message (Day 3-5, if accepted)
**Template:** "Thanks for connecting, [Name]. Quick context: I co-founded a strategy engine for insurance producers. It's a Jarvis-style tool that takes a 'I've got a guy' scenario and returns the full case design — strategy, pitch deck, COI workflow, wholesaler handoff. Several IMOs are using it white-label to drive recruiting, retention, and revenue. Worth a 15-minute call to see if it'd fit Peakbritt Financial Group's downline?"

**Compliance check:** Same as above. No pitch in the message — just an ask for a call.

### Step 4: Discovery Call (Day 7-14, if the message is responded to)
**What happens on the call:** Run the discovery call demo deck (see Three Pitch Decks). Show the engine in action. Walk through the white-label experience. Discuss the pilot offer.

**Length:** 30 minutes.

**Goal:** Pilot agreement, scheduled for the following month.

### Step 5: Pilot Agreement (Day 21-30)
**What happens:** Sign the pilot agreement. 50 seats for 90 days at discounted pricing. Co-launch support: kickoff call, training videos, in-app announcements.

**Goal:** Convert to standard pricing at month 4.

### Step 6: Expansion (Month 4+)
**What happens:** If the pilot is successful, the IMO expands to more seats (100, 500+). The IMO becomes a case study. The case study becomes recruiting material for the next IMO.

## The Qualification Criteria (How We Score Each Principal)

For each principal on the list, score on 5 criteria:

| Criterion | Score (1-5) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Downline size** | ___ | 25-100 agents = 1, 100-500 = 3, 500-1000 = 4, 1000+ = 5 |
| **Tech-forward** | ___ | Legacy stack = 1, modern CRM = 3, actively evaluating new tech = 5 |
| **Recruiting pain** | ___ | Stable downline = 1, losing agents = 3, actively trying to grow = 5 |
| **Decision-making authority** | ___ | Committee = 1, principal + 1 advisor = 3, principal alone = 5 |
| **Personal relationship / warm intro** | ___ | Cold outreach = 1, mutual connection = 3, direct relationship = 5 |

**Total score:** [Sum, max 25]

**Priority tiers:**
- **20-25:** Hot leads. Reach out within 1 week. Personalize aggressively.
- **15-19:** Warm leads. Reach out within 2 weeks. Standard personalization.
- **10-14:** Cool leads. Reach out within 4 weeks. Light personalization.
- **5-9:** Cold leads. Skip unless they're the right person for a specific reason.
